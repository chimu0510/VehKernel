# Local display and local menu: status always, whitelisted parameters only while disarmed

A board with a display and a user key shows the vehicle's status on the display and offers a local menu that changes parameters, so a vehicle can be checked and set up on the bench without a development host. The C50C V1.1 has both: an OLED on four GPIO lines and the user key S3 (board definition). The local menu is one more client of the parameter service (ADR-0012) and nothing else: it is never a source of setpoints, mode changes or arming requests, and nothing it does can make an actuator move. Betaflight's CMS menu is the model: it opens only while disarmed, and arming is refused while it is open.

**Local display**

- The local display subscribes to topics on the bus and publishes nothing the control chain reads. Producing modules never know it exists, as with the logger (ADR-0019).
- It runs on the lowest-priority work queue, never on `wq:ctrl`. A display on GPIO lines busy-waits for every byte, so it refreshes at a bounded rate and redraws only what changed; the timing monitor measures its cost (ADR-0015).
- **Graphics, not only text.** The display is a 128 × 64 monochrome image: a 5 × 7 font for text, a large digit font for key values, 8 × 8 icons and lines, rectangles, circles and bitmaps for gauges and bars. Fonts and icons are drawn for Vehkernel and kept as constant arrays in the source; no third-party font is taken in.
- **A status bar on every page and in the menu**, 8 pixels high: the mode, armed, the emergency stop, each Link and which one is in control, the remote control, a warning when an arming refusal is pending and a fault icon when a fault is recorded, and page dots.
- **Pages**, in this order: overview (large mode and arming state, source in control, emergency stop, actuator power, reset cause, last refusal or disarm reason, heading and speed); attitude (an artificial horizon, roll, pitch and yaw, the estimator and its rejected and fused counts); motion (forward speed, yaw rate, local position, and one bar pair per wheel of the frame, speed against target and output duty); arming checks (each check passing, failing or skipped, from the commander's `arming_checks` topic); sensors (the IMU and the magnetometer, and whether a magnetometer calibration is applied); Links (each Link's state, the one in control marked); remote control (stick positions, triggers, buttons pressed); system (firmware identity from `firmware_info`, board, uptime, reset cause and fault, the work item with the worst timing, parameter storage). Later milestones add their own pages, such as battery.
- **Refresh.** Pages refresh at most five times a second, the attitude page ten; what the key changes shows within 50 ms. Only the parts of the image that changed are sent. The display starts on the overview and never changes page by itself: a fault or a refusal raises the status bar's warning instead, so the image does not jump while someone drives.
- **Display protection.** A page that stays still marks an OLED. While disarmed, the display dims after `DSP_DIM_S` seconds without a key press (60 by default) and turns off after `DSP_OFF_S` seconds (600); while armed it stays on and bright. Every two minutes the whole image moves by one pixel along a four-step cycle (`DSP_SHIFT`), armed or not; layouts keep a one-pixel margin. `DSP_BRIGHT` sets the normal brightness. A key press on a dimmed or dark display only wakes it; arming or disarming, a mode change, the emergency stop engaging, a fault, an arming refusal and a takeover by the remote control wake it too.
- A display that is missing or does not answer is reported over a Link where its bus can tell; on a write-only bus, such as the C50C's, the firmware cannot know. Either way it affects nothing else: it is not part of verifying the board at boot (ADR-0010) and not an arming check.
- **Key gestures.** A click (released, and no second press within 250 ms) moves to the next page or item, a double click (two presses within 250 ms) confirms, and a long press (held 1 s, acting while still held) goes back. The 250 ms is a constant, and a press shows at once on the display, so the wait for a possible second press does not feel like lag.
- While armed the user key only switches pages: a double click shows that the menu needs the vehicle disarmed, and a long press returns to the overview.

**Local menu**

- **Only while disarmed.** The local menu opens only while the vehicle is disarmed, with a double click on any page.
- **Groups, then parameters.** The menu lists groups, a group lists its parameters, and a parameter opens its edit screen. A click moves to the next entry, wrapping around; a double click enters; a long press goes back one level, and at the groups closes the menu, back to the page it was opened from. The menu holds settings only: the pages are the information.
- **Arming is refused while it is open.** "Local menu closed" is an arming check that cannot be skipped (ADR-0005). The menu closes itself after 30 s without a key press, discarding any unconfirmed edit, so a menu left open on the bench does not block arming from a Link for long; the refusal reason tells the Link why. The 30 s is a constant.
- **One gate.** Every change goes through the same parameter service as a Link, with the same type, range and safety attribute checks. A rejection is shown with its reason. Each change publishes `parameter_update`, so every connected Link sees it, and storage follows ADR-0020; the vehicle is disarmed, so the spare sector may be erased.
- **A whitelist, not a blacklist.** Only parameters whose definition sets `menu = true` appear in the local menu, so a new parameter stays out of it until someone decides otherwise. A parameter group that drives actuators or holds arming checks, failsafe actions or setpoint timeouts declares `menu = false` for the whole file, and the parameter generator rejects `menu = true` inside it; today these are `MOT` and `COM`. Beyond that, a parameter enters the menu only when changing it neither changes a control loop nor weakens a safety rule. Every menu parameter names its group with `menu_group`, which the parameter generator requires with `menu = true`. The whitelist, by group: ATTITUDE (`EST_PRIMARY`, `ATT_KP`, `ATT_KI`, `ATT_ACC_REJ`), COMPASS (`EKF_MAG_TYPE`, `EKF_MAG_DECL`), MANUAL (`MAN_VX_MAX`, `MAN_YAW_MAX`, `MAN_DEADZONE`, limits that move nothing by themselves), VEHICLE (`WHL_RADIUS`, `WHL_TRACK`, which odometry and kinematics use), BOARD (`SENS_BOARD_ROT`), LINKS (`SERIAL1_BAUD`, `SERIAL2_BAUD`) and DISPLAY (`DSP_BRIGHT`, `DSP_DIM_S`, `DSP_OFF_S`, `DSP_SHIFT`). Control loop gains and limits (`RATE_`, `VEL_`), `RC_TIMEOUT`, the frame and wheel outputs (`FRAME_`, `WHL_*_OUT`), the magnetometer calibration (`CAL_MAG_`, written by calibration) and the EKF's noise and gates stay out.
- **Staged edits.** The edit screen shows the name, unit, range, the value now and the staged value, and a row of actions: `+`, `-`, `x10` (switching the step between one and ten `menu_step`), `DEF` and `SAVE` for a number; `NEXT`, `PREV`, `DEF` and `SAVE` for an enumeration. A click moves to the next action and a double click runs it; a long press discards the staged value. A value is written only by `SAVE`; each double click needs the key released first, so a key held down or stuck confirms at most once. Numeric parameters step by their `menu_step` within their range, which the parameter generator requires with `menu = true`; enumerations step through their value names. `SAVE` shows `SAVED` or why it was refused. When a Link changes a parameter the menu shows, the menu reloads it from `parameter_update`, and the last confirmed write wins.
- **Reset to default.** Each menu parameter can be reset to its default, through the same gate.
- **Reboot required.** A changed `reboot_required` parameter is marked as needing a reboot; the menu does not reboot the board.
- **Key handling.** The user key is debounced. A key that reads pressed at boot is ignored until it has been released.

**Not in the local menu**

Arming and disarming, mode changes, engaging or releasing the emergency stop, starting bring-up cases, anything that moves an actuator, rebooting, and resetting every parameter at once. Actions such as starting a calibration or restarting the estimators wait for their own decision.

**Firmware metadata**

The local menu shows each parameter by its name, which the firmware already holds. Enumeration value names are compiled into the firmware for menu parameters only; every other parameter keeps only what validation needs (ADR-0012). The whitelist bounds the Flash this costs.

**Board definition**

A display is a board device in the board definition (ADR-0011), with its controller type and peripheral bus, and the user key is a pin role. Board revisions that move display pins are separate board definitions (ADR-0010): the C50C V1.2 moved the display's reset and data/command lines.

## Considered Options

- **Arming, mode changes or the emergency stop from the local menu**: rejected. The menu would become a source in setpoint arbitration (ADR-0005), operated by one key with no second confirmation, by someone standing next to the wheels.
- **The user key as an emergency stop source**: not adopted for now. Engaging is the safe direction, but the key is shared with the menu, so a press would mean two things, and an on-board key is out of reach on a moving vehicle. Revisit with the remote control.
- **Editing while armed parameters that are not `locked_when_armed`**: rejected. Live tuning over a Link has a tool and an operator at a distance; the local menu has neither.
- **A blacklist of parameters the menu must not show**: rejected. Every new parameter would appear in the menu by default, and forgetting to list one fails unsafe.
- **Every parameter's enumeration names in the firmware**: rejected for the reasons in ADR-0012; only menu parameters need them on the board.
- **A reboot item in the menu**: not adopted in the first version. The menu marks what needs a reboot, and a power cycle does the rest.
- **A short press to move and a long press to confirm**, the first version's keys: replaced. Confirming by holding made every choice slow and left no gesture for going back; a double click confirms and a long press goes back, as on one-button devices.
- **Changing the page by itself on a fault or a refusal**: rejected. The image would change under someone driving; the status bar's warning says something needs a look.
- **Dimming or turning off the display while armed**: rejected. The status must stay readable while the vehicle moves; moving the image by a pixel protects it then.
- **Inverting the image periodically against marking**: rejected. It flickers, and moving and dimming the image protect the panel without it.
- **Resetting every parameter from the menu**: rejected. It would also reset parameters outside the whitelist, such as `MOT` and `COM`, which the menu must not change.
